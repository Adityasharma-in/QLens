# QLens — Launch Film Script

### "Don't Leave."

**Product:** QLens — Quick Search Overlay for Chrome  
**Master:** 1:40 approx. · 16:9 · 1080p60  
**Cutdowns:** 0:30 social · 0:15 vertical  
**Tagline:** The web, in focus.  
**Secondary line:** One keystroke. No trace.

> **Logline:** Every search used to mean leaving the page. This is the film about the search that doesn't.

---

## The idea

QLens is not a faster way to search. It is a way to search without leaving.

So the film does not begin with the product. It begins with the small, familiar cost: you're reading, a word stops you, and to look it up you have to pack up your attention and move. New tab. Paste. Search. Come back. Find your place.

Then QLens removes the departure. One shortcut. The word is already there. Enter. Results. Back. The page is exactly as you left it.

The film never says "productivity." It shows the relief.

## What the film has to prove

1. The overlay only appears when summoned.
2. The selected text is already in the box — no copy, no paste.
3. The page comes back clean. No bar, no panel, no residue.
4. The product is deep, but the film stays quiet: four engines, AI Mode, image search, math, the lost tab.
5. It is private by construction, not by promise.

## Where to use what

- **1:40 master** — launch page, YouTube, press, Product Hunt gallery.
- **0:30 cut** — Chrome Web Store listing video, paid social, X/LinkedIn.
- **0:15 vertical** — Shorts, Reels, TikTok.
- **0:06 bumper** — pre-roll, end screens, event loops.

**Picture**

A clean Chrome window. A long, warm editorial article, mid-scroll. The page is a purpose-built demo: **"The Quiet Chemistry of Rain."** One word is highlighted — **petrichor**. Hold on it for a beat, long enough to read.

Then the old ritual, cut fast and a little cold:

Ctrl+C. New tab. Ctrl+V. Enter. Results. Back. A small scroll up and down to find the place again. The tab strip goes from one tab to two and back. The article is the same. The moment isn't.

**VO**

> Every search begins with a small departure.
>
> You're reading. A word stops you. To look it up, you leave.
>
> New tab. Paste. Search. Come back. Find your place.
>

**On screen**

Nothing. Let the action read.

**Sound**

Room tone. A single low piano note, slightly detuned. Keyboard clicks dry and close. The new tab is a soft intake of air — not a whoosh.

## 03 — The return
**0:25 – 0:39**

**Picture**

Press Enter. Slow the moment to 50% for a beat: the overlay is gone, the page is clean, and only then does the new tab take over. This is real behavior, not an edit trick — QLens destroys the overlay before the tab is created.

Results page. Real, legible, no personal data. Hold two seconds.

Click back to the original tab. Same scroll. Same selection. No bar. No dim. No glow. Pan 20 pixels up and back down — proof of no residue. Close the results tab. The article remains, untouched.

**VO**

> Press Enter. Results open in a new tab.
>
> And when you come back — the page is exactly where you left it.
>
> Nothing to close. Nothing left behind.

**On screen**

Three short lines, bottom left, one at a time:

`No bar.`  
`No panel.`  
`No trace.`

**Sound**

Enter click. Overlay dismiss: a soft reverse tick. The results tab opens with air. When we return, the music drops to almost nothing — just room tone and one held note. The silence is the proof.

# Production notes

## Look

- Every frame of UI is the real extension. No mockups, no After Effects UI, no invented screens.
- Capture at 2× device scale, 60fps. Use a 1440×900 Chrome window at 100% zoom, bookmarks bar hidden, dedicated demo profile with only the tabs you want on camera.
- Build a demo article; don't use a real site. It avoids cookie banners, copyright, and personal data. Warm bone background, serif body, large title. Set `::selection` to a soft mint so the selected word belongs to the palette.
- If the options-page version badge is visible, make sure it matches the release you're launching — or keep it out of focus. Don't feature a version number that will age in a week.
- Suggested demo copy:

> **THE QUIET CHEMISTRY OF RAIN**
>
> Before it hits the ground, rain has already made a promise. The smell that follows — petrichor — is older than us.
>
> It comes from the ground, not the sky. A molecule called geosmin, produced by soil bacteria, meets the first drops and rides the air toward you. Your nose can find it at a few parts per trillion. That is a strange kind of superpower for a species that spends most of its time indoors.
>
> There is a second act, too. Ozone, split by lightning, drifts down before the rain arrives. That sharp, clean scent is the storm announcing itself. Petrichor is the storm remembering the ground.
>
> None of this makes the smell less strange. If anything, it makes the moment better: you are standing in a conversation between the sky and the soil, and you have been invited.

- Before capture day, test the Google results, AI Mode, and Lens paths end to end. If a surface is walled or unstable, use a clean working recording or cut the scene. Never show an error page.
- Dismiss cookie banners, consent dialogs, notification prompts, and "restore pages" bubbles before recording.
- Grade: neutral and true-to-life. A touch of warmth. No vignette. Don't sharpen UI text into aliasing.
- Old-way montage: −10% saturation, slightly cooler. When the overlay opens, restore color and warmth. The grade shift is the emotional turn.
- Live-action inserts: plain keyboard, warm neutral desk, soft window light, 50mm, shallow depth of field. Use the founder's hands if possible. No logos, no jewelry, no coffee cup.
- If you shoot on a Mac, show **Control**, not Command — the manifest shortcut is MacCtrl+Shift+K. Or use a PC keyboard for the insert so the keycaps match the VO.
- Text graphics: Inter. Bone `#F5F2EA` on obsidian `#0A0D12`. Mint `#4FE0B0` only for the dot or a single hairline. No gradients, no glows, no drop shadows.
- Don't add fake lens flares. The product's glass is the effect.

## Edit

- Cut on keystrokes, not music beats. The keyboard is the rhythm.
- Hold the clean page for a full beat. Let the silence land.
- Don't cut away before the viewer reads the selected word or the result.
- Every feature shot needs a beginning, middle, and end: open the overlay, do the thing, see the result.
- Keep feature shots moving; let the clean page and the end card breathe.
- Slow the Enter/return moment to 50% for a beat. Don't fake the overlay disappearing.
- Keep the cursor still. Hide it for keyboard-only shots. For clicks, move slowly and stop before clicking.
- Type at a natural speed. If a query is long, cut the middle of the typing — don't speed it up in post.
- The master is designed for sound-on. The 0:30 and 0:15 cuts carry the story in on-screen text for sound-off viewing.

## Sound

- Original score: felt piano, warm analog pad, subtle sub pulse, tape hiss. 70 BPM. No drums, no risers, no whooshes.
- Structure: one detuned note in the detour → resolves when the overlay lands → light pulse through the features → strips to one note and room tone for privacy → warm chord at the end.
- SFX: dry key clicks, a soft glass tick, a reverse tick on dismiss, a quiet paste thud, a one-note confirmation chime. Keep them low. The VO is the lead.
- Mix: −14 LUFS integrated, −1 dBTP. Dialogue forward. No music under the privacy line except a held note.
- If you license a track, avoid "corporate inspirational" builds and drops. The film should feel like a room, not a trailer.

## Voice

- Warm, low-mid, conversational. 135–145 wpm. Not an announcer.
- Keep the pauses. The script has them for a reason.
- Don't smile on the privacy lines. Don't sell the CTA. Just say it.
- Record three takes: conversational, slower, close-mic. Use the conversational one.
- If the founder records it, keep it. A slightly imperfect read is more believable than a polished one.
- De-ess, remove mouth noise, keep breaths. No reverb.

# What not to say

- Don't call it "AI-powered." AI Mode is a feature, not the product.
- Don't call QLens a search engine. It's the quick search overlay; the engine stays your choice.
- Don't say "works everywhere." It works on normal http(s) pages, not `chrome://`, the Web Store, PDFs, or the New Tab page.
- Don't call it a "productivity hack." It's a tool, not a life lesson.
- Don't say "private" while implying the search engine can't see your query. It goes to the engine you choose.
- Don't say it replaces the browser or the omnibox. It doesn't.
- Don't use "seamless," "effortless," "supercharge," "unlock," "revolutionary," "game-changer," "elevate," "empower," or "unleash."
- Don't invent statistics or testimonials.
- Don't show a persistent bar on the page. That is the opposite of the product.
- Don't add a whoosh. It's a search bar, not a spaceship.
